## In brief

After 21 days in milk (DIM), the most commonly studied reproductive conditions in dairy cows are purulent vaginal discharge (PVD) and endometritis, as they are associated with impaired reproductive performance. Although in both cases the diagnosis is made by measuring inflammation, intrauterine treatment with cephapirin (CEPH; Metricure, Merck Animal Health) has been shown to have a positive impact on the reproductive success of affected cows. However, the sensitivity of these tests in identifying uterine infection has recently been suggested to be low, while another diagnostic tool that can be used on the farm, the luminometer, has been shown to have better sensitivity. The main objective of this randomized controlled trial is therefore to identify the impact of CEPH on the reproductive success of cows with EVP, endometritis, and/or uterine infection. The secondary objective is to use an economic analysis to determine in which context(s) the implementation of a systematic approach to diagnosis and treatment would be financially beneficial for dairy farmers. This study will identify whether a subpopulation of cows with EVP and/or endometritis, i.e., those with uterine infection, benefit from CEPH, while those with inflammation only, i.e., without infection, do not need this treatment. This project is part of a vision for the judicious use of antimicrobials by improving the identification of cows with uterine infections. In addition, the economic analysis could support better adoption of treatment for affected cows, thereby improving the health of dairy herds.
